# MUSTER Comprehensive Glossary of Terms

> Also published in Notion as a child page of "MUSTER — Product Standard Operating Procedure".
> This file is the source of truth; edit here first, then re-sync the Notion page.

## Purpose

The MUSTER Glossary defines the terminology used across MUSTER assessments, scores, findings, controls, reports, recommendations, and SITREPs.

MUSTER evaluates more than whether a website works. It considers artificial intelligence, cybersecurity, domain and email trust, website assurance, privacy, accessibility, operational resilience, governance, compliance readiness, and organizational risk.

---

## A

- **Access Control.** Controls that determine who or what may access a system, resource, function, or data.
- **Accessibility.** Designing digital services so people with disabilities can perceive, navigate, understand, and use them.
- **Accessibility Conformance.** The degree to which a digital experience satisfies requirements such as WCAG success criteria.
- **Account Takeover.** Unauthorized control of a legitimate account.
- **Agent.** An AI-enabled system that can take actions, use tools, access information, or complete tasks.
- **Agentic AI.** AI capable of planning, selecting actions, using tools, and pursuing goals with some operational autonomy.
- **AI.** Artificial intelligence. Computational systems performing functions such as reasoning, prediction, classification, language processing, or content generation.
- **AI Accountability.** Clear responsibility for AI decisions, controls, oversight, outcomes, and corrective action.
- **AI Actor.** A person, team, organization, or system involved in the AI lifecycle.
- **AI Assurance.** Processes used to establish confidence that AI systems operate as intended and relevant risks are understood.
- **AI Governance.** Policies, responsibilities, controls, oversight, and decision structures governing organizational AI.
- **AI Inventory.** A documented list of AI systems, tools, models, vendors, integrations, automations, and use cases.
- **AI Lifecycle.** The stages of planning, designing, developing, testing, deploying, monitoring, modifying, and retiring AI.
- **AI Literacy.** Understanding AI capabilities, limitations, risks, responsibilities, and appropriate use.
- **AI Model.** A computational system trained or configured to generate, predict, classify, recommend, or analyze.
- **AI Policy.** Documented organizational rules for acceptable, restricted, prohibited, and required AI practices.
- **AI Readiness.** The organization's ability to adopt AI effectively, securely, responsibly, and sustainably.
- **AI Risk.** The possibility that AI use causes negative consequences for people, systems, operations, organizations, or society.
- **AI Risk Management.** Identifying, evaluating, treating, monitoring, and communicating AI-related risk.
- **AI System.** Technology using AI to produce content, predictions, recommendations, classifications, decisions, or actions.
- **AI Transparency.** The degree to which relevant information about AI purpose, limitations, operation, or use is understandable.
- **Algorithm.** A defined set of computational instructions.
- **API.** Application Programming Interface. A mechanism allowing software systems to exchange information or functionality.
- **API Key.** A credential used to identify or authorize an application accessing an API.
- **Application Security.** Protecting applications against unauthorized access, manipulation, vulnerabilities, disruption, or data exposure.
- **Asset.** Anything valuable to an organization, including systems, data, credentials, domains, applications, reputation, or intellectual property.
- **Attack Surface.** All potential systems, endpoints, interfaces, applications, services, and entry points available to an attacker.
- **Authentication.** Verifying identity.
- **Authorization.** Determining what an authenticated identity may access or do.
- **Availability.** The ability of systems, applications, information, or services to remain usable when needed.

## B

- **Backup.** A separate copy of information or system data maintained for restoration.
- **Backup Verification.** Confirmation that backups exist, are current, accessible, and reasonably restorable.
- **Benchmark.** A reference point used to compare current condition, performance, maturity, or risk.
- **Bias.** A systematic tendency that can produce unfair, inaccurate, distorted, or uneven results.
- **BIMI.** Brand Indicators for Message Identification. An email standard allowing participating providers to display verified brand logos when requirements are met.
- **Bot.** Software that performs automated tasks.
- **Broken Access Control.** A weakness allowing access beyond authorized permissions.
- **Broken Link.** A hyperlink pointing to an unavailable resource.
- **Business Continuity.** The ability to maintain essential operations during and after disruption.
- **Business Email Compromise.** Fraud involving impersonation or compromise of trusted business identities.

## C

- **Certificate.** A digital credential used to establish trust, identity, or encrypted communication.
- **Change Management.** A controlled process for reviewing, approving, documenting, implementing, and monitoring changes.
- **CIA Triad.** Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability.
- **Client-Side Security.** Security associated with code and content executed on a user's browser or device.
- **Cloud Service.** Remote infrastructure, storage, platforms, applications, or computing services.
- **Compliance.** Adherence to applicable laws, regulations, contracts, standards, frameworks, or policies.
- **Compliance Readiness.** The degree to which controls, documentation, evidence, and responsibilities support a compliance effort.
- **Confidentiality.** Protecting information from unauthorized disclosure.
- **Consent.** Permission for a defined collection, use, sharing, or processing activity.
- **Content Security Policy.** CSP. A browser control restricting allowed sources for scripts, images, styles, frames, and other content.
- **Control.** A safeguard, configuration, policy, process, or practice used to reduce risk or achieve an objective.
- **Control Effectiveness.** How successfully a control performs its intended function.
- **Cookie.** Browser-stored information used for authentication, preferences, analytics, personalization, or tracking.
- **Credential.** Information or cryptographic material used to establish identity or access.
- **Critical Finding.** A potentially severe condition that may require urgent investigation or remediation.
- **Cryptographic Failure.** Weak, absent, exposed, or improperly implemented encryption or cryptographic protection.
- **Cybersecurity.** Protecting systems, networks, applications, devices, and information from attack, disruption, manipulation, or unauthorized access.

## D

- **Data Classification.** Categorizing data based on sensitivity, value, confidentiality, or regulatory requirements.
- **Data Governance.** Policies and responsibilities governing how information is collected, stored, accessed, used, shared, retained, and deleted.
- **Data Integrity.** The accuracy, consistency, completeness, and trustworthiness of information.
- **Data Leakage.** Unauthorized or unintended exposure of information.
- **Data Minimization.** Collecting or processing only information necessary for a defined purpose.
- **Data Processing.** Any collection, analysis, storage, use, transmission, modification, sharing, or deletion of information.
- **Data Retention.** Rules governing how long information is maintained.
- **Data Subject.** An identifiable individual whose personal information is processed.
- **Dependency.** A service, software component, API, platform, or package another system relies upon.
- **Digital Footprint.** The collection of digital assets, systems, accounts, domains, content, and traces associated with an organization.
- **Digital Trust.** Confidence that an organization's digital systems, communications, identities, and practices are legitimate, reliable, secure, and responsibly managed.
- **DKIM.** DomainKeys Identified Mail. Email authentication using cryptographic signatures to help verify authorized sending and message integrity.
- **DMARC.** Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting and Conformance. An email authentication policy using SPF and DKIM alignment to help control treatment of unauthorized messages.
- **DMARC Alignment.** The relationship between the visible From domain and domains authenticated through SPF or DKIM.
- **DMARC Enforcement.** A DMARC policy directing supporting mail systems to quarantine or reject failing messages.
- **DMARC Monitoring.** Reviewing DMARC reports to identify authorized senders and authentication failures.
- **DNS.** Domain Name System. The system that connects domain names with internet services and technical records.
- **DNSSEC.** Domain Name System Security Extensions. Cryptographic validation designed to reduce certain forms of DNS manipulation.
- **Domain.** A human-readable internet address used for websites, email, applications, or services.
- **Domain Spoofing.** Impersonating a trusted domain.

## E

- **Encryption.** Transforming readable information into a protected form requiring authorized means to interpret.
- **Endpoint.** A device, system, service, URL, API, or network location communicating with another system.
- **Evidence.** Information demonstrating whether a control, process, activity, requirement, or condition exists.
- **Explainability.** The degree to which AI behavior, reasoning, factors, or outputs can be meaningfully explained.
- **Exposure.** A condition increasing the possibility that a threat can affect an organization or asset.
- **External Dependency.** A third-party system or service required for organizational operation.

## F

- **Fairness.** Consideration of unjustified differences in treatment, access, opportunity, or outcomes.
- **False Negative.** A test incorrectly indicating that an actual issue is absent.
- **False Positive.** A test indicating an issue that further investigation determines is not present.
- **Finding.** An observable condition requiring attention, review, validation, monitoring, or remediation.
- **Firewall.** A system controlling network traffic according to defined rules.
- **Foundation Model.** A broadly trained AI model that can support multiple tasks and applications.

## G

- **Generative AI.** AI capable of producing content such as text, images, audio, video, code, or synthetic data.
- **Governance.** Decision rights, responsibilities, policies, controls, accountability, and oversight.
- **Governance Gap.** Technology or operational activity that lacks adequate ownership, policy, controls, documentation, or oversight.
- **Guardrail.** A technical or procedural restriction designed to reduce unsafe, inappropriate, or unauthorized behavior.

## H

- **Hallucination.** AI output presenting fabricated, unsupported, inaccurate, or misleading information as reliable.
- **Header.** Metadata exchanged between servers and browsers.
- **High-Risk Finding.** A significant weakness or exposure requiring elevated attention.
- **HSTS.** HTTP Strict Transport Security. A mechanism directing browsers to use HTTPS.
- **Human-in-the-Loop.** A system design where humans review, approve, supervise, correct, or intervene in automated processes.
- **Human Oversight.** Meaningful human supervision of systems, decisions, outcomes, or risks.

## I

- **Incident.** An event affecting or threatening security, data, systems, operations, privacy, or availability.
- **Incident Response.** The process of identifying, containing, investigating, remediating, recovering from, and learning from incidents.
- **Injection.** A vulnerability where untrusted input is interpreted as commands or executable instructions.
- **Integrity.** The condition of systems or information remaining accurate, complete, authentic, and protected from unauthorized modification.
- **Internal Control.** A policy, responsibility, safeguard, procedure, or system designed to manage organizational risk.
- **Inventory.** A documented record of organizational systems, applications, AI tools, vendors, devices, domains, or assets.

## J

- **JavaScript.** A programming language widely used for interactive web functionality.
- **Justification.** Documented reasoning supporting a risk, exception, decision, control, or treatment.

## K

- **Key Risk Indicator.** KRI. A measurable signal indicating changing or increasing risk.
- **Key Performance Indicator.** KPI. A measurable indicator used to evaluate performance against an objective.

## L

- **Least Privilege.** Providing only the minimum access necessary.
- **Likelihood.** An estimate of how probable a risk event is.
- **Logging.** Recording events, activity, changes, errors, transactions, or access attempts.
- **LLM.** Large Language Model. An AI model trained on large volumes of data to process and generate language and related outputs.

## M

- **Machine Learning.** AI techniques that identify patterns in data to make predictions, classifications, recommendations, or outputs.
- **Malicious Code.** Software or instructions created to compromise, manipulate, monitor, damage, or disrupt systems.
- **Maturity.** The degree to which a capability is established, documented, repeatable, measured, governed, and improved.
- **MFA.** Multi-Factor Authentication. Authentication requiring more than one verification factor.
- **Misconfiguration.** An incorrect or insecure configuration increasing risk.
- **Mitigation.** Action taken to reduce risk likelihood or impact.
- **Model Drift.** Changes in model performance or behavior over time.
- **Monitoring.** Periodic or continuous observation for changes, failures, anomalies, or risk.
- **MTA-STS.** Mail Transfer Agent Strict Transport Security. A standard allowing domains to require supporting mail systems to use secure transport.
- **MX Record.** A DNS record identifying mail servers responsible for receiving email.

## N

- **NIST.** National Institute of Standards and Technology.
- **NIST AI RMF.** NIST Artificial Intelligence Risk Management Framework. A voluntary framework for managing AI risk.
- **Nonconformity.** A condition that does not satisfy a defined requirement or expectation.

## O

- **OAuth.** An authorization framework allowing applications limited access to resources without directly sharing passwords.
- **Open Redirect.** A weakness allowing a trusted application to redirect users to arbitrary destinations.
- **Operational Resilience.** The ability to withstand, respond to, recover from, and adapt to disruption.
- **OSINT.** Open-Source Intelligence. Information collected from publicly available sources.
- **OWASP.** Open Worldwide Application Security Project.
- **OWASP Top 10.** A widely recognized awareness standard describing major web application security risks.

## P

- **Patch.** A software update correcting defects, vulnerabilities, or other issues.
- **Patch Management.** The process of identifying, testing, deploying, and verifying software updates.
- **Personal Data.** Information relating to an identified or identifiable individual.
- **PII.** Personally Identifiable Information. Information that identifies or can reasonably identify a person.
- **Phishing.** Deception designed to obtain information, credentials, money, or harmful actions from a user.
- **Policy.** Documented organizational requirements, expectations, responsibilities, or rules.
- **Privacy.** Responsible collection, use, sharing, retention, and protection of information about individuals.
- **Privacy Notice.** A statement explaining how personal information is collected, used, stored, processed, or shared.
- **Privacy Risk.** The possibility that data processing causes adverse effects for individuals.
- **Processing Integrity.** The degree to which system processing is valid, complete, accurate, authorized, and timely.
- **Prompt.** Input, instructions, context, or information supplied to an AI system.
- **Prompt Injection.** Instructions intended to manipulate an AI system into ignoring intended behavior or exposing protected information.
- **Provenance.** Information describing the source, origin, ownership, transformation, or history of data or digital artifacts.

## Q

- **Qualitative Risk Assessment.** Risk evaluation using descriptive categories.
- **Quantitative Risk Assessment.** Risk evaluation using numbers, probabilities, costs, or measurable values.

## R

- **Ransomware.** Malicious activity designed to deny access to systems or information and demand payment or another concession.
- **Recovery.** Restoring acceptable operation after disruption or failure.
- **Red Teaming.** Structured testing simulating adversarial behavior, misuse, or attacks.
- **Remediation.** Corrective action taken to resolve or reduce a weakness or risk.
- **Residual Risk.** Risk remaining after controls or mitigation.
- **Resilience.** The ability to withstand, recover from, and adapt to disruption.
- **Risk.** The possibility that uncertainty, threats, events, or weaknesses negatively affect objectives, people, systems, or assets.
- **Risk Acceptance.** A documented decision to retain a recognized risk.
- **Risk Appetite.** The general amount and type of risk an organization is willing to pursue or retain.
- **Risk Assessment.** The process of identifying, analyzing, and evaluating risk.
- **Risk Register.** A record of risks, owners, severity, controls, status, and treatment.
- **Risk Tolerance.** The acceptable level of variation or exposure associated with a risk.
- **Risk Treatment.** Avoiding, reducing, transferring, sharing, or accepting risk.
- **RBAC.** Role-Based Access Control. Granting permissions based on assigned roles.

## S

- **SaaS.** Software as a Service. Hosted software delivered through the internet.
- **Security Control.** A safeguard protecting systems, information, operations, users, or assets.
- **Security Header.** An HTTP header instructing browsers to apply security protections.
- **Security Misconfiguration.** An insecure configuration exposing systems or information to unnecessary risk.
- **Sensitive Data.** Information requiring additional protection due to potential harm from misuse or exposure.
- **Session.** A temporary interaction state between a user and a system.
- **Shadow AI.** AI tools, accounts, systems, models, or automations used without proper authorization, visibility, or governance.
- **Single Point of Failure.** A component whose failure could disrupt an entire critical service or process.
- **SOC 2.** An assurance reporting framework for service organizations based on AICPA Trust Services Criteria.
- **Software Supply Chain.** The code, libraries, vendors, tools, packages, services, repositories, and infrastructure supporting software delivery.
- **SPF.** Sender Policy Framework. An email authentication mechanism identifying authorized sending systems for a domain.
- **SSL.** Common terminology for secure website encryption. Modern secure web connections use TLS.
- **Subdomain.** A subdivision of a primary domain, such as app.example.com.
- **Supply Chain Risk.** Risk introduced through suppliers, vendors, dependencies, contractors, infrastructure, or services.

## T

- **Technical Debt.** Future cost or operational difficulty caused by incomplete, temporary, outdated, or poorly maintained technical decisions.
- **Threat.** An actor, circumstance, event, or condition capable of causing harm.
- **Threat Actor.** A person, organization, group, or automated system capable of malicious activity.
- **Third Party.** An outside vendor, provider, contractor, platform, processor, or organization.
- **Third-Party Risk.** Risk introduced through external organizations or services.
- **TLS.** Transport Layer Security. The modern protocol protecting information transmitted across networks.
- **TLS-RPT.** TLS Reporting. A standard allowing domain owners to receive reports about secure email transport failures.
- **Tracking Technology.** Technology used to observe user activity, identifiers, behavior, or interactions.
- **Trust Services Criteria.** AICPA criteria covering Security, Availability, Processing Integrity, Confidentiality, and Privacy.

## U

- **Uptime.** The amount or percentage of time a service remains available.
- **Uptime Monitoring.** Automated observation of system or website availability.
- **URL.** Uniform Resource Locator. The address of a web resource.
- **User Consent.** Permission from a user for defined processing or collection activities.

## V

- **Validation.** Determining whether something fulfills its intended purpose.
- **Vendor.** An external provider of technology, infrastructure, software, consulting, data, or services.
- **Vendor Risk.** Risk arising from reliance on external vendors.
- **Verification.** Confirming that a control, configuration, system, or requirement meets expectations.
- **Vulnerability.** A weakness that can contribute to compromise, failure, or harm.
- **Vulnerability Assessment.** A systematic process for identifying and evaluating weaknesses.

## W

- **WAF.** Web Application Firewall. A system inspecting and controlling traffic to web applications.
- **WCAG.** Web Content Accessibility Guidelines.
- **WCAG 2.2.** A WCAG version containing testable accessibility success criteria.
- **Website Assurance.** Evaluation of website security, accessibility, trust, privacy, resilience, technical health, and operational controls.
- **Website Health.** The technical condition of a website, including availability, performance, security signals, errors, links, and certificates.
- **Website Monitoring.** Automated or scheduled observation of website availability, configuration, certificates, security, or performance.
- **Webhook.** A mechanism allowing one system to trigger actions or send information to another when an event occurs.

## X

- **X-Content-Type-Options.** A security header preventing browsers from interpreting files as unintended content types.
- **XSS.** Cross-Site Scripting. A weakness allowing malicious scripts to execute in content viewed by users.

## Z

- **Zero Trust.** A security approach requiring verification rather than automatically trusting users or systems.
- **Zero-Day Vulnerability.** A vulnerability that may be exploited before an effective patch is broadly available.

## MUSTER-Specific Terms

These are MUSTER-defined concepts. They should not be presented as definitions created by NIST, OWASP, AICPA, W3C, CISA, or another standards organization.

- **Assurance Benchmark.** A reference point used by MUSTER to compare observed conditions against expected assurance practices.
- **Assurance Finding.** A condition affecting trust, security, accessibility, governance, privacy, resilience, AI readiness, or another assessed area.
- **Assurance Layer.** A major MUSTER assessment category. Potential layers include:
  - AI Readiness
  - AI Governance
  - Cybersecurity
  - Website Assurance
  - Domain and Email Trust
  - Privacy
  - Accessibility
  - Operational Resilience
  - Compliance Readiness
  - Third-Party Risk
- **Assurance Posture.** The combined condition of assessed risks, controls, evidence, findings, and practices at a point in time.
- **Benchmark Gap.** The difference between an observed state and a target or benchmark state.
- **Control Confidence.** MUSTER's indication of how strongly available evidence demonstrates that a control exists and functions.
- **Control Coverage.** How much relevant risk is addressed by existing controls.
- **Digital Assurance.** MUSTER's umbrella concept for evaluating security, trust, accessibility, privacy, governance, resilience, and operational discipline.
- **Domain Trust.** The MUSTER assessment area covering domains, DNS, certificates, email authentication, encryption, and related trust controls.
- **Evidence-Backed Finding.** A finding supported by technical evidence, documentation, test results, or other verifiable information.
- **Evidence Confidence.** The strength and reliability of evidence supporting a finding.
- **Exposure Indicator.** A condition increasing potential exposure without proving compromise or harm.
- **Finding Confidence.** The level of certainty that an observed condition represents a valid finding.
- **Finding Severity.** The potential significance of a finding. Severity should remain distinct from remediation priority.
- **Governance Readiness.** The degree to which policies, ownership, documentation, accountability, controls, and oversight exist.
- **Known Finding.** A condition sufficiently supported by available evidence.
- **MUSTER.** A digital assurance and AI readiness platform designed to identify, organize, explain, prioritize, and track technology, trust, governance, accessibility, security, compliance-readiness, and operational risk.
- **MUSTER Assessment.** A structured evaluation using automated tests, observed evidence, organizational information, manual review, or combinations of those methods.
- **MUSTER Benchmark.** A defined reference condition used to compare organizational practices or technical conditions against expected practices.
- **MUSTER Finding.** An observable condition surfaced through MUSTER. A finding does not automatically mean:
  - a breach occurred
  - a law was violated
  - regulatory noncompliance exists
  - a cyberattack succeeded
  - negligence occurred
  - harm occurred
- **MUSTER Readiness.** The demonstrated ability to responsibly operate, govern, secure, monitor, and improve a capability.
- **MUSTER Risk Signal.** Technical, operational, or governance evidence suggesting further review may be warranted.
- **MUSTER Score.** A MUSTER-generated measurement representing assessed conditions across defined dimensions. A MUSTER Score is not government certification, regulatory approval, a formal audit opinion, or a guarantee of security unless an independent external process establishes such status.
- **MUSTER SITREP.** Situation Report. An executive-level view of current assurance posture, material findings, significant changes, priorities, and recommended actions.
- **Observable Evidence.** Information MUSTER can directly detect, retrieve, test, or substantiate.
- **Priority Finding.** A finding elevated because of severity, exposure, likelihood, business importance, context, or other relevant factors.
- **Readiness Gap.** The difference between current demonstrated capability and capability reasonably required for responsible implementation.
- **Readiness Score.** A MUSTER measurement representing preparedness within a defined area.
- **Recommended Action.** A proposed step to address, validate, investigate, mitigate, monitor, or document a finding.
- **Remediation Status.** The current state of corrective work. Suggested states:
  - Open
  - Under Review
  - Planned
  - In Progress
  - Awaiting Validation
  - Remediated
  - Accepted
  - Exception Approved
  - Not Applicable
- **Risk Context.** Business, legal, technical, operational, human, or environmental information affecting how a finding should be interpreted.
- **Risk Signal.** Observable information indicating possible risk or a change in risk. A risk signal is not proof of compromise or regulatory violation.
- **Score Transparency.** Showing the evidence, findings, factors, and weighting logic contributing to a MUSTER score.
- **Verified Finding.** A finding that has received additional validation.
- **Website Assurance Score.** A MUSTER measurement reflecting website-related security, trust, accessibility, technical health, privacy, resilience, and operational controls.

## Finding Severity

- **Informational.** Useful context or improvement information without meaningful standalone risk.
- **Low.** Limited exposure or operational impact.
- **Moderate.** A condition warranting attention because it may contribute to meaningful exposure or weakness.
- **High.** A significant condition requiring elevated remediation attention.
- **Critical.** A potentially severe condition requiring urgent investigation, validation, containment, or remediation.

Severity does not prove exploitation or harm.

---

## Confidence Levels

- **Low Confidence.** Evidence is incomplete, indirect, ambiguous, or requires more verification.
- **Moderate Confidence.** Evidence reasonably supports the finding but additional validation may help.
- **High Confidence.** Strong observable evidence supports the finding.
- **Confirmed.** Evidence has been sufficiently validated to establish that the condition exists.

---

## Remediation Priority

- **Immediate.** Address or investigate as soon as reasonably possible.
- **High Priority.** Place ahead of normal improvement work.
- **Planned.** Include within an established improvement cycle.
- **Monitor.** Continue observation and reassess as conditions change.
- **Accepted.** The organization knowingly retains the risk after review.

## AI Risk Terms

- **AI Use Case.** A defined business, operational, analytical, administrative, technical, or creative use of AI.
- **AI Vendor Risk.** Risk introduced by providers of AI systems, models, infrastructure, APIs, platforms, or data.
- **Automated Decision.** A decision produced wholly or partly through a computational system.
- **Automation Bias.** Excessive trust in automated recommendations or outputs.
- **Content Provenance.** Information describing where digital content originated and how it may have changed.
- **Deepfake.** Synthetic or manipulated media designed to realistically imitate a person.
- **Human Review.** Authorized human evaluation of AI outputs, recommendations, decisions, or actions.
- **Model Evaluation.** Testing AI behavior, safety, performance, limitations, reliability, or related characteristics.
- **Model Risk.** Risk created by poor performance, misuse, assumptions, bias, limitations, or model changes.
- **Responsible AI.** Practices addressing safety, security, accountability, transparency, privacy, fairness, reliability, and human impact.
- **Synthetic Content.** Content generated or substantially modified using AI or computational systems.
- **Trustworthy AI.** AI for which relevant characteristics such as safety, reliability, accountability, security, resilience, privacy, fairness, transparency, and explainability have been appropriately considered.

## Domain and Email Trust

- **Email Authentication.** Methods helping recipients determine whether messages are legitimately authorized.
- **Email Deliverability.** The ability of legitimate mail to reach intended recipients.
- **Email Spoofing.** Forging email identity information to impersonate another sender.
- **Lookalike Domain.** A domain resembling another closely enough to confuse users.
- **Parked Domain.** A registered domain not currently operating a substantive service.
- **Registrar.** An organization authorized to register and manage domains.
- **Registrar Lock.** Protection against unauthorized domain transfer or modification.
- **Sender Authentication.** Methods used to validate whether email was legitimately sent on behalf of a claimed domain.
- **Subdomain Takeover.** A condition where an attacker can control a subdomain pointing to an abandoned or unclaimed external resource.

## Website Assurance

- **404 Error.** A response indicating a requested resource was not found.
- **Canonical URL.** The preferred URL for content available through multiple addresses.
- **Core Web Vitals.** Google metrics evaluating webpage loading, responsiveness, and visual stability.
- **HTTP.** Hypertext Transfer Protocol.
- **HTTPS.** HTTP protected using TLS.
- **Redirect.** A mechanism automatically sending a request from one URL to another.
- **Robots.txt.** A file containing instructions for automated crawlers.
- **Sitemap.** A structured list of website URLs.
- **SSL Certificate.** Common terminology for the digital certificate supporting HTTPS. Modern implementations use TLS.
- **Uptime Incident.** A period when a website or service becomes unavailable or materially impaired.

## Accessibility

- **Alternative Text.** Text describing meaningful image content.
- **ARIA.** Accessible Rich Internet Applications. Attributes helping assistive technologies interpret interfaces.
- **Assistive Technology.** Technology such as screen readers, magnifiers, voice controls, or alternative input devices.
- **Contrast Ratio.** The numerical difference between foreground and background colors.
- **Keyboard Accessibility.** The ability to operate functionality without a mouse or touch interface.
- **Screen Reader.** Software converting interface information into speech or another accessible format.
- **WCAG Conformance Level.** A, AA, or AAA groupings of WCAG success criteria.

---

## Privacy

- **Data Controller.** An entity determining why and how personal information is processed within legal frameworks using that terminology.
- **Data Processor.** An entity processing information on another entity's behalf.
- **Data Breach.** Unauthorized access, disclosure, loss, acquisition, alteration, or exposure meeting an applicable legal, contractual, or policy definition.
- **Privacy by Design.** Integrating privacy considerations into systems and processes from the beginning.
- **Tracking Pixel.** A small resource used to record content views or other user activity.

---

## Operational Assurance

- **Configuration Drift.** Unplanned differences between expected and actual system configurations.
- **Disaster Recovery.** Processes and technologies used to restore systems and operations after major disruption.
- **Mean Time to Repair.** MTTR. Average time required to restore operation following failure.
- **Recovery Point Objective.** RPO. The maximum tolerable amount of data loss expressed in time.
- **Recovery Time Objective.** RTO. The target time for restoring a service after disruption.
- **Single Source of Truth.** An authoritative information source intended to reduce conflicting versions.

## Critical MUSTER Distinctions

- **Assessment is not certification.** MUSTER can evaluate observable conditions without constituting formal certification.
- **Readiness is not compliance.** Strong readiness does not replace a legal, regulatory, contractual, or independent compliance determination.
- **A vulnerability is not a breach.** A weakness does not prove exploitation.
- **A finding is not a violation.** A technical or governance finding does not automatically establish a legal violation.
- **Security is not privacy.** Security protects systems and information. Privacy concerns appropriate handling of information about people.
- **Website security is not domain trust.** A secure website can still have weak DNS, email authentication, domain registration, or related trust controls.
- **Authentication is not authorization.** Authentication establishes identity. Authorization determines permitted actions.
- **Monitoring is not remediation.** Detection does not fix the problem.
- **AI adoption is not AI readiness.** Using AI does not prove that governance, training, oversight, security, or risk management exists.
- **AI governance is not AI prohibition.** Governance establishes boundaries, responsibilities, oversight, and controls for appropriate use.
- **A score is not a guarantee.** No MUSTER score guarantees security, compliance, accessibility, resilience, or freedom from risk.

## Core MUSTER Principle

Every material finding should answer:

1. What did we find?
2. Why does it matter?
3. What evidence supports it?
4. What should the organization do about it?
5. How will we know when it has been addressed?

MUSTER's objective is not to generate more findings.

Its purpose is to turn technical and organizational evidence into understandable decisions, prioritized action, and measurable improvement.
